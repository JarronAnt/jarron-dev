Phase 6 was a meaningful shift for ASCII Universe. Before construction, the core gameplay loop could remove terrain and move extracted resources around. Now the player can **use those resources to create new world geometry**. The interesting part is that walls and stairs are not placed instantly: autonomous workers have to make them happen.

## A construction plan is a promise, not a wall

The player places a plan, not a finished structure. The published pipeline is:

```text
Place a ConstructionPlan
          |
          v
   Determine requirement
          |
          v
     Reserve an item
          |
          v
   Create delivery job
          |
          v
   Hauler carries item
          |
          v
    Create build job
          |
          v
   Builder performs work
          |
          v
     Update GameMap
```

The distinction is fundamental. A UI click records intent; the simulation decides when that intent can become reality. Pending plans remain visible as construction ghosts.

## Reservations prevent two workers from claiming one resource

A construction plan marks a suitable item with `ReservedForConstruction`. Ordinary stockpile hauling cannot claim that item, and another wall remains waiting if no other suitable item exists. This is a small but essential concurrency-style problem in a single-threaded simulation: multiple autonomous systems must not assume they own the same piece of state.

Once the material reaches a work site, a builder claims a `BuildConstruction` job. This separates transportation from building and makes the responsibilities of workers explicit.

## Different structures, one world model

Phase 6 supports **walls, floors, ramps, up stairs, down stairs, and up/down stairs**, with stone or wood materials. The delivered item determines the final material, so a granite stone creates a granite structure, while an oak log creates an oak one.

The built result modifies the same `GameMap` used by terrain and pathfinding, not a secondary decorative building layer. Adding stairs or walls can therefore change where workers can travel.

## Deconstruction needs history

Removing a building is not always equivalent to setting its tile to empty. The `BuiltConstruction` record retains information about the original tile's geometry, material, features, and liquid state. That makes it possible to restore the previous tile when deconstructing and recover the appropriate material.

This is a good example of a design decision that is easy to skip early but hard to retrofit after save files already exist.

## Persisting unfinished work

Phase 6's **save version 3** persists construction plans, states, reservations, deliveries, progress, and built structures, with version 2 migration support. Loading a fortress should not lose the fact that a particular stone was already promised to a particular build site.

## What's next

Workshops will reuse many of the same ideas: reserve inputs, deliver items, schedule specialist labor, track progress, and materialize outputs. In that sense, construction is not just a gameplay feature; it is a foundation for industrial simulation.
